Just value (market value)
The Property Appraiser's estimate of what a home was worth on January 1. It is set once a year from past sales, so it often runs below what the home would sell for today. It is not an asking price or an appraisal.
Assessed value
Just value after any cap on yearly increases. For a long-time owner it can be far below just value. For a new owner it starts over at just value.
Taxable value
Assessed value minus exemptions. This is the number the millage rate is applied to.
Save Our Homes cap
Florida limits how fast a homesteaded home's assessed value can rise: 3% a year or inflation, whichever is less. The cap ends when the home sells, and the buyer is assessed at full just value from the next January 1. That is why a seller's tax bill says little about yours.
Homestead exemption
A reduction in taxable value for a home you own and live in as your permanent residence. You have to apply for it with the Property Appraiser, by March 1 of the year after you buy. It also starts your own Save Our Homes cap.
Millage rate
The tax rate, in dollars per $1,000 of taxable value. 18 mills on $300,000 of taxable value is $5,400 a year. It is the sum of the county, school board, city and other levies for the home's tax district.
Non-ad valorem assessments
Flat charges on the tax bill that don't depend on the home's value, such as stormwater, fire or street lighting fees. Exemptions don't reduce them.
Qualified sale
A sale the county treats as a fair sign of market price: an arm's-length deal between unrelated parties. Family transfers, foreclosures and sales of several parcels at once are not qualified.
Comparable sales (comps)
Recent sales of similar homes nearby, used to judge whether a price is reasonable. Agents and appraisers pick theirs by hand and adjust for condition. The ones here are picked by a rule and not adjusted.
Mortgage insurance (PMI and MIP)
Insurance that protects the lender, paid by you. A conventional loan with less than 20% down carries PMI until you have enough equity. An FHA loan carries MIP: a fee added to the loan at closing plus a monthly charge that usually lasts for the life of the loan.
Escrow
An account your lender keeps to pay your property tax and insurance. You pay a twelfth of each with every mortgage payment, and the lender collects a few months up front at closing.
Cash to close
Everything you pay on closing day: the down payment, plus closing costs, plus prepaid insurance and the first escrow deposit. It is more than the down payment, often by several percent of the price.
Documentary stamps and intangible tax
Florida taxes paid at closing. On a mortgage, the buyer pays 35 cents per $100 borrowed plus 0.2% of the loan. There is also a stamp tax on the deed, 70 cents per $100 of the price, which the seller usually pays.
Title insurance
A one-time policy against problems with ownership, such as an old lien or a missing heir. The lender's policy is required with a mortgage. The owner's policy protects you. Florida sets the rates, and the contract says who pays for which.
Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure
Two standard forms from your lender. The Loan Estimate comes within three business days of applying and lists the rate, payment and closing costs, so you can compare lenders line by line. The Closing Disclosure has the final numbers and must reach you three business days before closing.
FEMA flood zone
FEMA's rating of flood risk on its insurance map. Zones starting with A or V are Special Flood Hazard Areas, where a lender must require flood insurance. V zones also face waves. Shaded X is moderate risk and plain X is minimal. Homeowners insurance doesn't cover flooding in any zone.
Evacuation zone
The county's storm-surge zones, A through E, used to order evacuations before a hurricane. A leaves first. It is a separate map from FEMA's flood zones and doesn't set insurance requirements.
Four-point and wind mitigation inspections
Two short inspections Florida insurers ask for. A four-point covers the roof, electrical, plumbing and air conditioning, and is commonly required on older homes. A wind mitigation report records storm protections such as roof straps and shutters, and can lower the premium.
Milestone inspection and reserve study
Florida requires condo buildings of three or more stories to pass a structural inspection as they age, and to study and fund reserves for major repairs. A building that is behind can mean a large special assessment for owners.
HOA or condo fee
A monthly or quarterly charge from a homeowners or condo association. Lenders count it as part of your housing payment. The county doesn't record it, so take it from the listing and confirm it with the association.
