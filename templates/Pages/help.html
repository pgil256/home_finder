The steps, in order

  1. 1. Start from a monthly payment, not a price

    Decide what you can pay each month for the mortgage, property tax, insurance and any association fee together. In Pinellas, tax and insurance can add a third or more on top of the loan payment. On Explore the market, the filters turn a monthly budget into a price range.

  2. 2. Look into assistance before you shop

    Most down payment programs require a homebuyer class and one of their approved lenders, and some have to be in place before you sign a contract. Finding out afterward is too late.

  3. 3. Get pre-approved, by more than one lender

    A pre-approval letter tells sellers you can close. Apply with two or three lenders in the same two weeks and compare their Loan Estimates line by line. Credit checks for a mortgage that are made close together count as one.

  4. 4. Find homes on a listing site, then look each one up here

    This site has no listings or asking prices. Type an address to see the property tax you would pay as the new owner, what the home and similar ones sold for, the flood and evacuation zones, and the last roof permit. Save the ones you like to compare them side by side.

  5. 5. Get insurance quotes before you offer

    Ask an insurance agent for a homeowners quote and a separate flood quote on the exact address. The agent will want the roof's age and material. For a condo or a home in an association, ask for the fee, the budget and any special assessments that are planned.

  6. 6. Make an offer and use the inspection period

    Your contract gives you a set number of days to inspect and, under most contracts, to walk away. Book a home inspection straight away, along with the four-point and wind mitigation reports your insurer will ask for. Check with the city or county that permits for recent work were closed.

  7. 7. Close

    Your lender sends a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Compare it with your Loan Estimate and ask about anything that changed. Bring cash to close, which is more than the down payment. Confirm wiring instructions by phoning the title company on a number you looked up yourself, because fake wiring emails are a common fraud.

  8. 8. File for homestead

    The homestead exemption is not automatic. Apply with the Property Appraiser by March 1 of the year after you buy. How to file

What's different in Florida

The seller's tax bill isn't yours

Florida caps how fast a homeowner's assessed value can rise, so someone who has owned for twenty years can pay a fraction of what a new owner would. The cap ends at the sale. From the next January 1 you are taxed on the county's full just value. The tax shown on a listing is the seller's. Each home on this site shows an estimate for you.

Homestead takes an application{% if amendment_3_status == 'pending' %}, and the rules are on the ballot{% endif %}

If you live in the home, the homestead exemption takes $25,000 off its taxable value for every levy, and about another $26,000, adjusted for inflation each year, for everything except school taxes. It also starts your own cap on yearly increases. {% if amendment_3_status == 'pending' %} Amendment 3, on the November 3, 2026 ballot, would raise the non-school exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028 if 60% of voters approve it. People who become Florida residents after January 1, 2027 would wait five years for the larger amount. Estimates on this site show current law, with the amendment as a second figure. {% elif amendment_3_status == 'passed' %} Amendment 3, approved by voters in November 2026, raises the non-school exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. People who become Florida residents after January 1, 2027 wait five years for the larger amount. {% endif %}

Insurance can decide whether a home is affordable

Premiums vary widely with the roof's age, how the home is built and how close it is to the water. Insurers can require an inspection of a roof that is 15 years old or more, and some turn down older roofs. Two homes at the same price can differ by thousands of dollars a year, so quote the address before you offer.

Flood insurance is a separate policy

Homeowners insurance doesn't cover flooding. In a FEMA flood zone starting with A or V, your lender will require a flood policy. Outside those zones it is optional and usually cheaper, and homes there flood too. Ask the seller whether the home has flooded.

Evacuation zones are a different map

The county's evacuation zones are about storm surge and who is told to leave before a hurricane. A home can be in an evacuation zone without being in a FEMA flood zone, and the other way round. Mobile and manufactured homes are ordered out for any hurricane. Find a zone on the county's site

Older condo buildings carry new costs

Florida now requires condo buildings of three or more stories to pass a milestone structural inspection at 30 years, or 25 in some coastal areas, and to fund reserves for structural repairs. Ask the association for the inspection report, the reserve study, the reserve balance and any pending special assessments before you offer.

Closing costs include state taxes

Florida charges documentary stamps and an intangible tax on a mortgage, and sets title insurance rates statewide. The calculator on each home's page includes them in cash to close.

Help with a down payment

These programs lend money for a down payment and closing costs, usually as a second mortgage with no interest and no monthly payment. Most have income and price limits, and they open and close as funding runs out. The amounts below were checked in October 2026. Each program's own page has the current terms.

  • Florida Housing Finance Corporation

    The state's first-time buyer mortgages and down payment loans, including Florida Assist and Hometown Heroes. You apply through a participating lender, not with the state.

  • Housing Finance Authority of Pinellas County

    A first mortgage with a $10,000 no-interest second loan for the down payment and closing costs. For people who haven't owned a home in three years, and veterans.

  • Pinellas County Down Payment Assistance

    A no-interest, deferred loan of up to $75,000 for lower-income buyers, for homes in unincorporated Pinellas and the cities the county serves. Your lender applies for you.

  • City of St. Petersburg, City of Clearwater and City of Largo

    These three cities run their own programs for homes inside their limits, in place of the county's.

  • HUD-approved housing counselors

    Free or low-cost advice from nonprofits, and the homebuyer class most programs require. A counselor can tell you which programs you qualify for and whether they can be combined.

About the numbers

Values, sales, permits and parcel details come from the Pinellas County Property Appraiser's public files, refreshed monthly. Flood zones and flood claims come from FEMA. The mortgage rate is Freddie Mac's weekly average.

Everything here is an estimate from public records, and this guide is general information, not legal, tax, lending or insurance advice. Use it to ask better questions of your agent, lender, insurer and inspector.
